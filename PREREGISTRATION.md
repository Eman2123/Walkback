## Post-hoc diagnostic of the default result (exploratory, logged after the result was known)

The registered default (roughness 0.5, confidence 0.9) gave an empty calibrated region and 0.0 % coverage. This section records the explanation and what was and was not changed.

- What was found: the roughness factor is also applied to the habitat cell. At the default setting the habitat cell reaches the slope cutoff in 2 of 16 realizations (12.5 %), which is more than 1 minus the confidence (10 %). In those realizations nothing is reachable, so no cell can reach the 0.9 threshold. The empty region comes from this, not from the terrain far from the habitat.
- What was NOT changed: the model used for the headline, the default setting, and reports/validation_report.md. The registered result stays a SHORTFALL.
- What was added: an optional switch (compute_run pin_start, default off) and scripts/start_cell_diagnostic.py, which writes reports/start_cell_diagnostic.md for the same 9 combinations as the sensitivity analysis. With the start cell kept passable, the default setting gives 86.9 % coverage. That is still below 95 %, and it is still reported as a shortfall.
- Status of this table: post-hoc and exploratory. It explains the headline and does not replace it. Any future model change that fixes the start-cell behaviour needs its own registration before it is run.

## Planner-slope variant (exploratory, registered 2026-11-15 BEFORE the variant was run)

Observation that motivates it (found on the committed tile, before any variant result existed): the planner DEM is 100 constant 200 m blocks drawn on the 20 m grid. Slope computed with 20 m spacing is exactly 0 inside a block and large only on block edges (every cell above 0.5 deg lies on a block edge; mean 7.7 deg on edges, 0.0 deg inside; the 20 m truth has about 4 deg in both places). So the registered planner slope is a staircase artifact, not the slope a 200 m model has.

What does not change: the registered default, the headline in reports/validation_report.md, and the API.

Variant "grid200": slope is computed on the 10 x 10 grid of 200 m block means, with 200 m spacing, and each value is repeated over its 20 m cells. Everything else is identical (speed model, cutoff, 16 realizations, seed 1234, truth slope at 20 m).

Registered analysis: the same 9 combinations as the sensitivity analysis (roughness 0.1, 0.3, 0.5 by confidence 0.5, 0.7, 0.9), habitat (50, 50), budget 900 s, speed 0.5 m/s (assumed), cutoff 20 deg (assumed). For both the registered model and grid200 report: deterministic, calibrated and truth areas, coverage with its Wilson interval, Brier score, and a secondary metric, false reach = share of calibrated cells that are not reachable in the truth. Coverage alone cannot detect a planner that is too optimistic, so it is never reported without false reach.

Claims rules: all 9 rows are reported for both models; no row is chosen as the result; if no row reaches 95 % coverage, say so. grid200 is not promoted to the default without its own registration. The scale mismatch (planner at 200 m, truth at 20 m) is the object of study, not a bug to hide.

Output: reports/planner_slope_variant.md, generated by scripts/planner_slope_variant.py.

## Steep-window validation (registered 2026-11-15 BEFORE any steep window was built or analysed)

Why: the Jezero window used so far has 0.2 % of 20 m cells at or above the 20 deg cutoff, so it cannot separate a good planner from a poor one (see the planner-slope section). This registers how steeper windows are chosen, before any of them is looked at.

Selection rule (fixed here, not tuned afterwards): scripts/find_steep_windows.py on the 1 m HiRISE Jezero DTM, with its defaults. Slope is taken on the 20 m block-mean grid (the truth grid), cutoff 20 deg, windows of 100 x 100 cells (2 km) stepped by 25 cells (500 m), no nodata, habitat cell (the window center) below the cutoff, share of cells at or above the cutoff between 2 % and 60 %, windows may not overlap, top 3 by share (mean slope breaks ties). The 60 % ceiling is there because a window where almost nothing is reachable is as uninformative as one where everything is.

Analysis on each selected window: the same 9 roughness x confidence combinations, habitat (50, 50), budget 900 s, speed 0.5 m/s (assumed), cutoff 20 deg (assumed), for both the registered model (staircase) and grid200. Report coverage with its Wilson interval, false reach, Brier score and the no-skill Brier of that window.

Claims rules: all 3 windows and all 9 rows per model are reported. No window is dropped or replaced after its result is seen; if the script returns fewer than 3 windows, report that number. If a window fails to build (nodata), say so.

Limits stated in advance: the windows are selected using the truth slope, so these results describe terrain chosen to be informative, not a random sample of Jezero or of any other terrain. The DTM is one site. Speed, cutoff and roughness remain assumptions.

## Extended validation and sub-grid roughness (exploratory, registered 2026-11-15 BEFORE any of it was run)

Why (found by reviewing the code and measuring on the committed tile, before any result below existed):
- The registered roughness factor is multiplicative (slope x exp(roughness x z)). A cell the planner sees as nearly flat can never be made steep by it. On the Jezero tile at roughness 0.5, 16 of the 20 truth cells at or above 20 deg have a staircase planner slope below 4.46 deg (the level a 3-sigma factor still cannot lift to 20 deg); for grid200 it is 18 of 20. Only at roughness 1.0 does grid200 reach all of them.
- The reliability diagram checks the probability of a steep cell, but the calibrated region is built from the probability of reaching a cell. The quantity behind "confidence" has never been checked against the truth.
- The Wilson interval treats cells as independent. Cells are spatially correlated, so it is probably too narrow.
- The travel-time solver is an 8-connected Dijkstra, not fast marching (docs said fast marching). Measured on flat ground at uniform speed: path length is up to 8.2 % above the straight-line distance, and the 900 s reach area is 0.575 km2 against 0.636 km2 for the true circle (-9.6 %). This is documented, not changed.

What does not change: the registered default, the headline in reports/validation_report.md, the API output, and every number of the registered model. Before any code change a hash of the full result was saved for 45 combinations (registered model, grid200 and pin_start variants, Jezero tile and the synthetic tile). They must be identical after the change; if one differs, the change is reverted.

Added metrics (additive, reported for every row):
1. Brier skill score = 1 - Brier / no-skill Brier. At or below 0 means no skill.
2. Reliability of the probability of reaching a cell (p_reach, from the 16 realizations) against the truth reachable set, 10 bins.
3. Coverage and false reach as a function of the confidence threshold (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95), roughness 0.5.
4. 95 % block-bootstrap interval for coverage: the tile is cut into 10 x 10 cell blocks (200 m), blocks are resampled with replacement, 2000 resamples, seed 0, 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Shown next to the Wilson interval. When one interval is needed the wider one is quoted.

Variant "hurst" (exploratory, opt-in, needs planner slope mode grid200). The planner sees only the 200 m DEM and a scale exponent H. G200 is the RMS gradient of the 10 x 10 grid of 200 m block means. The predicted RMS gradient at 20 m is G20 = G200 x 10^(1 - H). The unresolved part per gradient component is sigma = sqrt(max(G20^2 - G200^2, 0) / 2). In each realization the gradient vector of the 200 m model is repeated over its 20 m cells, independent N(0, sigma^2) noise is added to each component of each 20 m cell, and the slope is arctan of the gradient length. The roughness parameter is not used. Everything else is as in the registered model (16 realizations, seed 1234, speed model, cutoff, habitat cell not pinned).
- H = 0.853, read from reports/scale_effect.md. It was measured on the same 2 km window that is validated, so this variant is IN-SAMPLE. It is labelled so in every output. No out-of-sample claim is made until H comes from a different window.
- Stated in advance: the noise is Gaussian and thin-tailed, while the truth is heavy-tailed (0.2 % of cells reach 20 deg although the RMS gradient is about 0.09). The variant may therefore still miss most steep cells. It is reported either way.

Runner: scripts/run_validation.py runs, on every tile in data/processed, the registered model (staircase), grid200, and grid200 + hurst. The multiplicative models use the same 9 roughness x confidence combinations (habitat (50, 50), budget 900 s, speed 0.5 m/s assumed, cutoff 20 deg assumed). The hurst model has no roughness, so it has 3 rows (confidence 0.5, 0.7, 0.9). Output: reports/validation_extended.md and reports/validation_extended.csv.

Claims rules: every row of every model is reported; no row is chosen as the result; the registered headline stays a SHORTFALL unless its own numbers say otherwise; a variant is not promoted to the default without its own registration; if no row reaches 95 % coverage, say so. The Jezero tile is one window of one terrain class, so none of this is a claim about other terrain.

Deferred on purpose: direction-dependent speed (uphill and downhill) with a reverse "walk back" solve, because the coefficients need a confirmed source and none is invented here; and a 16-neighbour or fast-marching solver, which would change the registered numbers and needs its own registration.

## Solver and directional speed (exploratory, registered 2026-11-15 BEFORE the code or any result)

These are the two items the previous section deferred on purpose. Both are opt-in. The registered default, the headline in reports/validation_report.md, the API output and every number of the registered model do not change. Before any source file changes, a hash of the full result was saved for 54 combinations (Jezero tile and the synthetic tile; registered model, grid200 and pin_start; 9 roughness x confidence combinations each). They must be identical after the change, and again at the end; if one differs, the change is reverted.

Solver "dijkstra16" (opt-in). Same Dijkstra, with 8 more moves (the knight moves, step = cell x sqrt(5)). A knight move is allowed only if both cells it passes beside are passable, so it cannot cut a corner through an impassable cell. Edge speed is the mean of the two end cells, as before. Fast marching is not added (it needs scikit-fmm, which was dropped because it fails to build on Windows). Stated in advance: on flat ground at uniform speed the worst path error should fall from 8.2 % to about 2.6 %, and the reach area (900 s) should move closer to the true circle than the registered -9.6 %. This is checked on a flat uniform grid first and reported.

Speed model "minetti" (opt-in) adds direction to the speed. The registered speed depends only on slope magnitude. Here the speed on an edge from cell u to cell v is the registered symmetric edge speed times f(g), where g = (z_v - z_u) / h is the signed grade along the edge (h = horizontal step length, g clipped to -0.45 to 0.45), and f(g) = Cs(g) / C(g), with C the metabolic cost of walking and Cs(g) = (C(g) + C(-g)) / 2 its symmetric part. C is the fifth-order polynomial of Minetti et al. 2002, J Appl Physiol 93:1039-1046, fitted to treadmill walking between -0.45 and +0.45:
C(g) = 280.5 g^5 - 58.7 g^4 - 76.8 g^3 + 51.9 g^2 + 19.6 g + 2.5 (J per kg per m).
The coefficients were read from the paper's PDF (iris.unibs.it copy). The extraction dropped the signs; they were checked by computing C(+0.45) = 17.6 and C(-0.45) = 3.6 against the measured 17.33 and 3.46 given there. f(0) = 1, so on flat ground the result equals the registered one. Assumptions, all labelled in every output: (1) speed is inversely proportional to cost (constant metabolic power); (2) the data are Earth treadmill walking, with no Mars gravity, suit or load factor; (3) the signed grade uses the planner elevation for the planner and the ensemble (not perturbed by roughness) and the 20 m fine elevation for the truth; (4) the slope cutoff and the speed reduction by slope magnitude are unchanged. Because f is a ratio to the symmetric part, this adds direction only and does not re-fit the magnitude.

Direction "out" (registered) is the time from the habitat to each cell. Direction "back" is the time from each cell to the habitat, which is what "walkback" means: Dijkstra from the habitat over reversed edges, so an edge is costed in the direction the crew walks it. With a symmetric speed the two are equal; with "minetti" they differ. In "back" mode the calibrated region, the truth region and the deterministic region are all the cells from which the return fits in the budget.

Runner: scripts/run_solver_directional.py runs, on every tile in data/processed, the models staircase (registered) and grid200, each with solver {dijkstra8, dijkstra16} x {linear out, minetti out, minetti back}, over the registered 9 roughness x confidence combinations (habitat (50, 50), budget 900 s, speed 0.5 m/s assumed, cutoff 20 deg assumed). Reported per row: coverage with Wilson and block-bootstrap intervals, false reach, areas, Brier, Brier skill score. Output: reports/solver_directional.md and .csv. Every row is reported.

Stated in advance: this window has 0.2 % of 20 m cells at or above the cutoff and mostly small grades, so f stays close to 1 and the "minetti" results are expected to differ little from the registered ones; the size of that difference is the finding, not a safety claim. The window is one site; the steep windows chosen by scripts/find_steep_windows.py are on the owner's computer and not part of this run.

Claims rules: no row is chosen as the result; no variant is promoted to the default without its own registration; a difference between "out" and "back" is reported even when it is zero.
